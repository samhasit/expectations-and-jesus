Before the group meets

Prepare

  • Read John 7:25–44 and John 12:12–19 yourself, slowly, in context.
  • Listen to the first podcast episode if you can. The discussion guide links both.
  • Choose three or four of the ten group questions that fit your group. Question 1 and question 10 work as bookends.
  • Ask two people ahead of time to read John 7:25–44 and John 12:12–19 aloud.
  • Decide whether the group watches the discussion guide on a screen or follows on their phones.

The flow

Eight steps, forty-five minutes

Minute marks are a guide, not a rule. The stopwatch highlights the step you should be in. Every step ends with what the group is looking at on the discussion guide, so you never have to leave this page.

  1. 0:00 – 3:00

    Open

    Say
    Tonight's question is simple to ask and hard to live with: what happens when Jesus is faithful, but he does not do what we expected?
    Do
    Pray briefly. Ask God to show the group who Jesus actually is, not who we assumed he would be.
    Transition
    "Let's read the first scene. Listen for what the crowd thinks it already knows about Jesus."

    What happens when Jesus is faithful, but he does not do what we expected?

    Jesus offers living water.

    Scene two John 12:12–19

    Jesus enters as a different kind of King.

  2. 3:00 – 10:00

    01 · Two scenes

    Set the scene
    John 7:25–44. It is the Feast of Tabernacles in Jerusalem, about six months before the cross. Jesus has come up to the feast quietly and is now teaching openly in the temple courts. The city is divided. Some want him arrested, some wonder if he is the Christ, and the leaders have sent officers to seize him. The argument keeps returning to where Jesus comes from, because the crowd assumes it already knows.
    John 12:12–19. Five days before Passover, the day after Mary anointed Jesus at Bethany. Word has spread that he raised Lazarus, so the festival crowd pours out of the city waving palm branches and shouting the words of Psalm 118, the welcome for a conquering king. Jesus answers by riding in on a young donkey. John admits the disciples only understood it afterward.
    Read
    John 7:25–44, then John 12:12–19. Two readers, no commentary between them.
    Say
    The people around Jesus were not simply uninformed. Many used true titles and biblical ideas, but fitted Jesus into a mission they had already chosen for him.
    Ask
    What did the crowds expect from Jesus, and how did those expectations prevent them from understanding him?
    If the room is quiet
    "What labels does the crowd put on Jesus in each passage? Where does each label come from?" Notice that the words are often right and the mission is wrong.
    Transition
    "The crowds had a job description for Jesus. So do we. Let's find ours."
    What the group sees

    01 / Two scenes

    The right words. The wrong expectations.

    The people around Jesus were not simply uninformed. Many were using true titles and biblical ideas, but fitting Jesus into a mission they had already chosen for him.

    John 7

    Too ordinary to recognize

    The crowd focuses on what it thinks it knows about Jesus: where he came from, where he might go, and whether he fits its picture of the Messiah. Jesus keeps pointing beyond their categories to the Father who sent him and the Spirit he will give.

    John 12

    The King they tried to define

    The crowd welcomes Jesus with royal language, yet his humble entrance points to a victory larger than political relief. He will defeat sin and death through suffering, resurrection, and peace.

    Discuss together

    What did the crowds expect from Jesus, and how did those expectations prevent them from understanding him?

  3. 10:00 – 15:00

    02 · Expectation audit

    Do
    Ask everyone to pick the expectation that feels most familiar: comfort, control, timeline, success, or relief. On phones they tap it. In a room, a show of hands works.
    Say
    Expectations are not automatically wrong. They become dangerous when a desire quietly turns into a promise Jesus never made.
    Ask
    Which expectation is easiest to disguise as mature faith?
    Transition
    "Now let's look at what Jesus actually reveals about himself in these two scenes."
    What the group sees

    02 / Expectation audit

    What role have you assigned to Jesus?

    Expectations are not automatically wrong. They become dangerous when a desire quietly turns into a promise Jesus never made.

    01Keep me comfortableIf Jesus loves me, life should get easier.

    When tapped

    Comfort is a gift, not the measure of his love.

    Jesus cares about suffering, but he never makes an easy life the proof that we belong to him. The cross prevents us from equating love with the absence of pain.

    A better question Can I trust Jesus’ love without using comfort as the evidence?

    02Keep me in controlI need to know how the whole plan will unfold.

    When tapped

    Trust does not require seeing the whole route.

    We may ask Jesus for guidance while quietly demanding control. Faith can move forward with enough light for the next faithful step, even when the ending remains hidden.

    A better question What would trust look like if I never received the full explanation?

    03Act on my timelineA good answer should also be an immediate answer.

    When tapped

    Delay is not the same as indifference.

    A delayed answer can feel like a verdict on God’s care. Scripture gives us language for waiting, lament, and persistent prayer without promising that every good desire will arrive on our schedule.

    A better question Have I turned my preferred timing into a test of God’s goodness?

    04Validate my successIf this fails, God must have failed me.

    When tapped

    The cross changes how we recognize victory.

    Jesus’ apparent defeat became the place of his triumph. Visible success can be a gift, but failure, weakness, or loss cannot automatically tell us whether God is present and at work.

    A better question Am I measuring faithfulness by results Jesus never promised?

    05Remove every hardshipSuffering must mean something has gone wrong.

    When tapped

    Jesus meets a need deeper than immediate relief.

    It is right to ask for rescue from suffering. Jesus also frees us from sin and death, gives his Spirit, and promises resurrection, gifts no temporary change can replace.

    A better question Can I ask boldly for relief while receiving the deeper rescue already given?

    Talk about it

    Which expectation is easiest to disguise as mature faith?

  4. 15:00 – 22:00

    03 · A different King

    Set the scene
    Zechariah 9:9–10. Written to a small, discouraged community of returned exiles in Judah, with the temple in ruins and no king on David's throne. Zechariah promises a coming King who is righteous and victorious yet humble, riding a donkey rather than a war horse, whose rule will bring peace to the nations. John 12 quotes this passage directly.
    Colossians 2:9–15. Paul writes from prison to a young church in Colossae that is being told Jesus is not enough, that they also need extra rules, visions, or spiritual powers. His answer is that the fullness of God lives in Christ, and that at the cross Jesus cancelled our record of debt and disarmed every rival power, putting them to open shame. The cross was the victory.
    Read
    Zechariah 9:9–10. If time allows, add Colossians 2:9–15.
    Walk through
    The four contrasts on the guide: impressive figure versus the Son in ordinariness, worldly power versus a humble King, immediate relief versus rescue from sin and death, visible victory versus the cross.
    Ask
    Why do you think humility, suffering, and the cross were so hard to recognize as victory?
    If the room is quiet
    "What would you have expected a king entering the capital to look like? What does a donkey say instead?"
    Transition
    "When Jesus does not fit our script, disappointment follows. That is where we go next, and we go there honestly."
    What the group sees

    03 / A different King

    Jesus is greater than the rescue we imagined.

    In both passages, people see something true about Jesus but reduce his mission to categories they can already understand.

    We may expect

    Jesus reveals

    A figure impressive enough to fit our picture of spiritual authority

    →

    The Son sent by the Father, present in surprising ordinariness

    A leader who takes power in the way the world recognizes

    →

    A humble King whose rule brings peace to the nations

    Immediate relief from the pressure we can see

    →

    Rescue from sin and death, the enemy beneath every other enemy

    Visible victory without weakness, waiting, or loss

    →

    Victory through the cross, resurrection, and self-giving love

    John 7:25–44 John 12:12–19 Zechariah 9:9–10 Colossians 2:9–15

    Jesus does not offer a better version of the life we scripted. He offers himself, and a kingdom larger than our script.

    Discuss together

    Why do you think humility, suffering, and the cross were so hard to recognize as victory?

  5. 22:00 – 29:00

    04 · When God disappoints us

    Say
    Unmet expectations become discontent, resentment, anxiety, or a sense of betrayal. Those feelings are not a reason to hide. They help us name what we believed Jesus was supposed to do.
    Walk through
    Name the loss. Trace the expectation. Compare it with what was promised. Bring it to Jesus. Lament and surrender can coexist.
    Ask
    What do your emotions toward God reveal about what you expect from him?
    Care
    Someone may be carrying real pain tonight. Do not rush past it or resolve it for them. Jesus cares deeply about suffering, injustice, grief, and loss. The deeper rescue does not make those wounds trivial.
    Transition
    "Jesus does not answer disappointment with a smaller promise. He answers with himself."
    What the group sees

    04 / When God disappoints us

    Disappointment can reveal the promise we assumed.

    Unmet expectations can become discontent, resentment, anxiety, or a sense of betrayal. Those emotions are not a reason to hide. They can help us name what we believed Jesus was supposed to do.

    01

    Name the loss

    What did you hope would happen? Do not minimize real pain or pretend the outcome does not matter.

    02

    Trace the expectation

    What did you assume this outcome would prove about God, yourself, or your future?

    03

    Compare it with promise

    Did Jesus actually promise this result, or did a good desire quietly become a demand?

    04

    Bring it to Jesus

    Honest lament and surrender can coexist. Faith does not require pretending you are not disappointed.

    Hold this carefully

    Jesus cares deeply about physical suffering, injustice, hunger, grief, and oppression. The deeper rescue he brings does not make those wounds trivial. It means our circumstances, however painful, do not have the final word about his love or our future.

    Discuss together

    What do your emotions toward God reveal about what you expect from him?

  6. 29:00 – 34:00

    05 · Living water

    Set the scene
    John 7:37–39. The last and greatest day of the same feast as scene one. Each morning of Tabernacles the priests carried water from the pool of Siloam and poured it out at the altar, remembering God's provision in the wilderness and praying for the promised Spirit. On that day Jesus stands up and cries out that anyone who is thirsty should come to him. John explains that he means the Spirit, given after Jesus is glorified.
    Isaiah 58:11. Isaiah is confronting people who fast and keep religious routines while ignoring the hungry and the oppressed. God promises that a life poured out for others will itself be satisfied: guided, strengthened, like a watered garden and a spring whose waters never fail. The picture Jesus reaches for in John 7 is already here.
    Read
    John 7:37–39 and Isaiah 58:11.
    Say
    Temporary gifts are still gifts, but none can carry the full weight of our hope. Jesus meets the deepest need: bringing us to God, forgiving sin, making us alive, giving his Spirit, securing resurrection.
    Do
    Read the six promises on the guide aloud, one person each. Point out the surrender tool as something to work through alone this week.
    Transition
    "Let's open it up. I have picked a few questions, but go wherever the group needs to go."
    What the group sees

    05 / What Jesus gives

    From temporary relief to living water.

    At a festival celebrating God’s provision, Jesus directs thirsty people toward a source that will not run dry: his own life and the promised Spirit.

    Temporary gifts are still gifts. A changed circumstance, restored relationship, new opportunity, or season of rest can be received with gratitude. But none can carry the full weight of our hope.

    Jesus meets our deepest need by bringing us to God, forgiving sin, making us alive, giving his Spirit, and securing resurrection life. His presence can sustain us before a circumstance changes, after it changes, or when it does not change at all.

    What is already true in Christ?

    01

    You are deeply loved.

    02

    Your sins can be forgiven.

    03

    You are made alive with Christ.

    04

    The Spirit is present and at work.

    05

    Resurrection is your promised future.

    06

    Jesus remains King in suffering and flourishing.

    John 7:37–39 Isaiah 58:11 Colossians 2:9–15

    Practice surrender

    Working through an unmet expectation?

    1. 01What did I expect?Name the outcome clearly.

      What outcome did I expect, and what did it represent to me?

    2. 02Was it promised?Separate desire from promise.

      Can I find this exact outcome in Jesus’ promises, or did I infer it from what I hoped faith would produce?

    3. 03What am I trying to control?Look beneath the demand.

      What uncertainty, vulnerability, or pain am I trying to avoid controlling?

    4. 04What is already true?Return to the gospel.

      What is already true because of Jesus, even if this situation remains unresolved?

    5. 05What is my faithful next step?Move without demanding certainty.

      What would trust, obedience, honesty, or love look like in the next small step?

  7. 34:00 – 42:00

    06 · Group discussion

    Do
    Use three or four of the ten questions. Discussion Mode on the guide shows them one at a time on a screen. Follow-ups are below each question in case the first answer is short.
    Suggested set
    Question 1 (the expectation we hold without noticing), question 5 (the story we tell when prayer seems unanswered), question 7 (how suffering exposes expectations), question 10 (what Jesus may be asking you to surrender).
    Keep it moving
    When an answer lands, ask "who else?" before moving on. When the group circles, name the tension and go to the next question.
    Transition
    "One statement and one last question, then we will close."
    What the group sees

    06 / Group discussion

    Let’s talk about it.

    Choose the questions your group needs most, or enter Discussion Mode to show them one at a time.

    01

    What expectation of Jesus is easiest to hold without realizing it?

    How might that expectation shape the way you interpret prayer, success, or suffering?

    02

    Can correct theology coexist with distorted expectations?

    What is the difference between knowing a true statement about Jesus and actually allowing that truth to redefine your hopes?

    03

    What does the crowd in John 7 think it knows, and what does it miss?

    Where do confidence, familiarity, or incomplete biblical knowledge make us less curious about who Jesus really is?

    04

    Why does Jesus enter Jerusalem as a humble King?

    How does the donkey challenge ordinary ideas about power, leadership, and victory?

    05

    When prayer seems unanswered, what story do you tell yourself about God?

    Do you tend to conclude that God is distant, displeased, powerless, or simply late? What feeds that conclusion?

    06

    What is the difference between asking Jesus for help and reducing him to a problem solver?

    How can we ask boldly for help while remaining open to a form of faithfulness we did not predict?

    07

    How can suffering expose what we expected from Jesus?

    Which expectations only become visible when something important is delayed, denied, or taken away?

    08

    Why can immediate relief feel more compelling than resurrection hope?

    What makes a future promise feel less concrete than a present circumstance, and how might the church help?

    09

    How does the promise of living water change the way we face disappointment?

    What practices help you receive strength from God’s presence instead of waiting for circumstances to supply it?

    10

    What expectation might Jesus be asking you to surrender?

    What would it look like to release that expectation without pretending the desire itself does not matter?

  8. 42:00 – 45:00

    07 · Takeaway and close

    Say
    Jesus is not failing when he refuses to fit the role we assigned him. What if disappointment with Jesus is sometimes an invitation to meet the Jesus who is actually there?
    Ask
    Do you want Jesus himself, or mainly the life you hoped he would provide? No answer out loud is needed.
    Do
    Close in prayer. Thank God for what is already true in Christ. Ask him to reshape the group's desires this week.
    Last line
    "Look again. Let Jesus be greater than your expectations."
    What the group sees

    07 / Final takeaway

    Jesus is not failing when he refuses to fit the role we assigned him.

    The crowds tried to understand Jesus through familiar categories: origin, geography, status, political power, and immediate relief. Jesus kept drawing their attention to the Father, the cross, the Spirit, resurrection, and a kingdom for every nation.

    What if disappointment with Jesus is sometimes an invitation to meet the Jesus who is actually there?

    When expectations are exposed

    2. 02 Read Jesus in the full context of Scripture.
    3. 03 Distinguish a good desire from a promised result.
    4. 04 Bring disappointment to God honestly.
    5. 05 Receive what Christ has already done.
    6. 06 Ask the Spirit to reshape your desires.

    Do you want Jesus himself, or mainly the life you hoped he would provide?

If you only have 30 minutes

The short version

  • Read John 7:25–44 and John 12:12–19, then skip straight to the audit as a show of hands (8 minutes).
  • Read Zechariah 9:9–10 and discuss only the "different King" question (7 minutes).
  • Walk the four disappointment steps without the group question (5 minutes).
  • Read John 7:37–39 and the six promises (4 minutes).
  • Discuss questions 1 and 10 only, then close (6 minutes).
